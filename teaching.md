The Philadelphia Flight Control Lab serves both the **Faculty of Aerospace Engineering** and the **Faculty of Mechanical Engineering**, providing laboratory space and experimental equipment for control education. Undergraduate students from both departments take compulsory and elective courses related to control systems engineering, with laboratory components held in the PFCL.

Below is an overview of the lab-oriented course offerings hosted at PFCL.

---

## Faculty of Aerospace Engineering

### Dynamic Systems (084737)
The undergraduate course *Dynamic Systems* introduces students to the mathematical foundation for modeling and analyzing dynamical systems. An important component of this course revolves around lab activities hosted in the PFCL. 

The course includes three laboratory modules aimed at bridging the gap between theoretical understanding and practical application:
* Exploring the limits of linearization when modeling dynamical systems.
* System identification of a DC motor using input-output data from experimental runs.
* Time and frequency domain response analysis of physical actuators.

### Control Theory (084738)
The undergraduate course *Control Theory* introduces students to the basic principles of feedback control. Students learn classical controller design methods, including PID control and lead-lag compensation.

The PFCL serves as a testing ground for these control designs:
* Designing and tuning controllers for physical DC motor testbeds.
* Stabilizing a rotary inverted pendulum apparatus.
* Analyzing the performance, stability margins, and disturbances on physical hardware.

### Advanced Control Lab (085705)
The *Advanced Control Lab* is for undergraduate students who wish to dive deeper into control design methods for real-world robotic systems. The course revolves around the design of control and estimation algorithms for the autonomous flight of a quadcopter drone using dedicated testbeds.

Students progress through:
* Deriving a complete non-linear dynamical model for the drone.
* Sensor calibration (IMU, optical flow, ultrasonic/barometric altimeter).
* Designing complementary filters and state estimators for multi-sensor fusion.
* Multi-loop controller implementation (attitude, velocity, altitude).
* Culminating in an autonomous flight tasked with following a sequence of 3D waypoints.

### Senior Design Projects (Project 7/8)
Project 7/8 is the capstone senior design project that all students within the Aerospace Faculty complete. This is a team-oriented systems engineering project often formed in collaboration with industry partners.

Recent capstone projects hosted at PFCL include:
* **Cornetto:** Interceptor missile designed as a reusable platform for studying onboard guidance algorithms and ground tracking systems.
* **AEOLUS (Autonomous Jet Powered UAV):** Integration of a micro-jet engine onto an autonomous airborne platform capable of autonomous takeoff, waypoint navigation, and landing with a 15+ minute flight endurance.

## Faculty of Mechanical Engineering

### Introduction to Control (034040)
This course introduces basic principles and tools for designing feedback controllers of dynamical systems. An integral part of the course takes place in the PFCL, where students design, implement, and validate their controllers on the DC motor testbeds.

### Advanced Control and Automation Lab (034406)
Advanced experimental laboratory covering multi-variable control, digital implementation, and automated experimental testbeds.

---

## Laboratory Testbeds & Equipment

* **Quadcopter Flight Arena:** Indoor netting cage with tracking for autonomous multi-rotor flight tests.
* **Rotary Inverted Pendulums & DC Motor Stations:** Dedicated hardware stations for feedback control experiments.
* **TurtleBot & Ground Mobile Robots:** Differential drive platforms for multi-agent swarm and cooperative navigation research.
* **3-Axis Moked Flight Table:** High-precision hardware-in-the-loop motion simulation.
